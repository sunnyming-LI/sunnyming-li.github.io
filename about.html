About

I am an architecture graduate and research assistant at Tongji University. I received my Bachelor of Architecture in July 2026 and now work at the university’s College of Architecture and Urban Planning.

My interest in computational design grew through making and testing physical structures. In OriTriO, I worked on a single flat panel assembly that folds into a chair, a stool, and a bench. Subsequent projects in deployable structures and robotic assembly led me to examine how geometric design, structural analysis, and fabrication affect one another.

My current research focuses on computational path generation for willow weaving. I combine structural information, material limits, and fabrication constraints in a workflow for generating and comparing finite-length rod paths. This work involves defining design objectives and constraints, implementing computational search, and examining the resulting alternatives.

My longer-term research goal is to improve or reshape practical design workflows through computation. I want to study how findings from analysis and fabrication can guide revisions to geometry, material choices, and construction strategies. This also involves reconsidering when decisions are made, how stages are connected, and which steps should be revisited as practical conditions change. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027 to pursue these questions.
