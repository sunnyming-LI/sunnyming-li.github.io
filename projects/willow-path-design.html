Willow Path Design

Multi-objective optimization of finite-length willow paths

This project investigates how finite-length willow rods can form weaving paths that account for structural information and material limits. A computational search produces feasible layouts and compares trade-offs in material distribution, alignment, bending, and rod count. Material calibration and physical execution of the optimized layouts remain to be validated.

Workflow

From Reference Fields to Weaving Paths

  1. Design

    Choose a planar outline and discretize it with irregular triangles, equilateral triangles, or quadrilaterals.

  2. Analysis

    Topology optimization, cleanup, and scoring provide node rewards. Reference stress and tensor extension provide directional guidance.

  3. Optimization

    Four objectives are evaluated under hard fabrication constraints. Local edits, repair, and ALNS update feasible and repair populations before strict checks and Pareto archiving.

  4. Translation

    Rod and node order become indexed light sequences. A board, guide pegs, layered rods, and removable caps prepare the workspace.

  5. Construction

    Four demonstrated primitives support a dual-arm system using camera views, task language, robot state, policy inference, and action retrieval.

Optimization

Four-Objective Willow Path Optimization

Structural Analysis

The mesh fixes supports and loads. Filtered and projected topology density becomes a material-distribution reward. Signed reference stress distinguishes tension and compression; analysed and extended axes guide edge orientation. Extension estimates guidance beyond the retained region without an additional FE solve. Later development separates fine-grid field analysis from the coarser weaving graph.

Additional structural analysis views (1)

Optimization Framework

Initialization classifies rod sets into feasible and repair populations. Modification, rebuilding, local edits, and ALNS are followed by strict checks. Only strictly feasible non-dominated candidates enter the archive; display selection occurs after search.

  1. Initialization
  2. Feasible + repair populations
  3. Local edits + regional rebuilding / ALNS
  4. Strict constraint checks
  5. Feasible non-dominated archive

Hard Constraints

The example covers 164 mandatory nodes and allows at most 20 unique optional nodes. Rods are simple open paths with no reused edges, branches, or cycles; shared nodes join two rod ends only. Each rod has at least one edge. Geometric length is at least 30; material length is geometric length + 40 and at most 1500, reserving 20 per end. Internal curvature is at most 1 and turning at most 100°. End and joint permissions apply, and prescribed ends stay exposed. Lengths use model units; curvature uses inverse model length.

Four-Objective Trade-offs

Pareto dominance uses the raw vector (−T, −A, B, k): no objective may be worse, and at least one must be better. Display weights are applied only after search.

System Integration

Path Translation + Weaving Setup

Willow Panel

Removable guides locate layered rods on an indexed 15 × 15 board. Locking caps secure the assembly and allow it to be released.

Experimental Setup

Two AM-ARM200 Pro follower arms, a mobile base, and a motorized lift form the workspace. A LAN connection links the PC client and Pi host; LeRobot supports recording, training, and evaluation.

Path-to-Light Control

Path encoding becomes an LED index and sequence, then board activation. The original examples retain the lighting sequence, UNO R3 and LED drivers, control interface, and LED-board prototype.

Robot System Framework

Chest, front, and wrist cameras provide views and LED target cues. Scene state combines images, task language, and joint/gripper readings. VLA/ACT inference and an action library feed policy selection and the command interface; motion paths, currents, and positions provide feedback.

  1. Camera views + task language + robot state
  2. VLA / ACT inference + action library
  3. Policy selection + command interface
  4. Motion + current + position feedback

Task Primitives & Training Records

The records show material picking, upper winding, lower winding, and pressing from chest, front, and left-wrist views. Picking uses 50 episodes (500–800 frames each); upper and lower winding use 200 each (1000–1600); pressing uses 50 (900–1200).

Future Research

From Geometric Paths to Validated Weaving

Layered Assembly

Use the single-layer Pareto archive as a library. Combine complementary paths and assess coverage, alignment, and rod allocation together with layer thickness, connections, and interference.

Material Calibration

Measure effective length, diameter, moisture, and defects. Calibrate bend and joint behavior, and test guide-rod deflection under staged contact loads.

Robotic Process

Coordinate tool clearance, intermediate willow shape, pulling demand, grip capacity, and weaving order. The Bandy2 tool is a process-study reference, not a completed implementation.
