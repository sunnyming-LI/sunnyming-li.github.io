Curve Folding

Curve-crease shell fabrication using developable approximation

Curve Folding connects shell form-finding with developability optimization and unfolding. The study improves geometric residuals while controlling changes to the input form. My work covers parametric modeling, Kangaroo form-finding, workflow integration, and output preparation.

Developability Optimization

From Optimization to Material Exploration

Optimization Objective

Minimize E(V) = Edev + λEshape, with Edev = EGauss + Eplanar and shape control from smoothness, isometry, and fold-angle terms. The aim is to improve developability while retaining the intended form.

minVEGauss+Eplanar+λsEsmooth+λiEiso+λfEfold

Energy Definitions

Gaussian curvature drives interior angle sums toward 2π; quad planarity drives faces toward planes. Smoothness controls boundary bending, isometry preserves reference edge lengths, and the hinge term preserves crease angles. The fold/diagonal alternative instead compares diagonal lengths. Geometric symbols & term definitions

Interior vertex angles follow adjacent edge vectors.

cos(θ1i)=(vj−vcloi)T(vk−vcloi)‖vj−vcloi‖2‖vk−vcloi‖2

The reference normal is the normalized mean of the corner normals.

n¯fq=norm(ni+nj+nk+nm4)

Corner normals are computed from adjacent edges.

ni=(vj−vi)×(vm−vi)

Bending vectors describe changes along a sidewall line.

uj=vi−2vj+vk,uk=vj−2vk+vm

Isometry compares current vertices with reference vertices ṽ. Ē and D̄ are the reference mean edge and diagonal lengths. The hinge term compares the cosine of each crease angle with its reference value; the diagonal expression is an alternative fold term.

Iterative Optimization

Initialize the input vertices, evaluate the five energy terms, take a gradient step, and repeat until convergence. The output is the optimized mesh V*, F, E.

  1. Initialize input vertices
  2. Evaluate five energies
  3. Gradient step
  4. Convergence check
  5. Optimized mesh V*, F, E
