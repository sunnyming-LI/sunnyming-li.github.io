OriTriO

Origami-inspired triple furniture from one sheet

OriTriO combines a chair, stool, and bench in a shared crease system. Posture dimensions guide the panel geometry; folding simulation and vector-based graphic statics (VGS) inform revisions before the system is detailed and built with MDF panels.

Team

Workflow

A Pipeline from Observation to Fabrication

  1. Design Research

    Origami rules and ergonomic dimensions establish the initial folding and seating requirements.

  2. Crease Iteration

    Chair, stool, and bench postures are translated into a common grid; visual references and simulation guide crease refinement.

  3. Folding Analysis

    VGS identifies mechanisms; a shared structural unit is revised and checked through physical paper models.

  4. Joint Detailing

    Thick-fold and full-fold strategies are compared alongside hinge types and panel strength, density, and finish.

  5. Fabrication

    The parametric creases, simulation, and structural model feed panel solids, CNC cutting, and assembly.

Site observations establish three posture types. Contact points, body dimensions, and load conditions translate gathering, waiting, drawing, meditation, picnicking, and seating into folding grids and angle ranges.

Computer Simulation Process

Crease settings and constraints are defined in Crane/Kangaroo. Sampled folded states provide both final-form geometry and kinematic data.

AI Generation

Reference images, LoRA training, and multiple prompts generate preliminary silhouette and support references. These images support visual exploration; the crease system is resolved through geometric study and simulation.

Hinge Research

Thick-fold treatment and three full-fold strategies address panel thickness. The selected bi-groove hinge combines MDF panels with Oxford fabric so a single crease can support different folding directions.

Future Research

Towards Lighter, Smarter, and More Responsive Structures

Inflation & Origami

Variable air pressure is proposed alongside rigid or functional panels. Two paths combine form and VGS with tension, compression, and inflation to study stability, transformability, and portability.

Cable-Actuated Folding

Vertex trajectories, crease motions, and panel kinematics would inform cable actuation for single-action, rapid deployment.

Developed Structures Research

Deployability optimization links form with motion planning, morphological control, and fabrication integration for curved spatial structures.
